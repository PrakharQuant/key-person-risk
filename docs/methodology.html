Methodology

Full technical details and formulas are in METHODOLOGY.md.

Event-study design

  • Market model estimated on trading days [−250, −30]; require ≥120 observations.
  • Abnormal return = actual − predicted. Cumulative abnormal returns (CARs) over [−1,+1], [0,+5], [0,+20].
  • Winsorization, cross-sectional t-tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, and non-parametric tests.
  • Day-0 alignment rules for after-close and non-trading-day announcements.

Key Person Risk Index (KPRI)

0–100 score per sector combining severity of the average CAR, tail risk (10th percentile), hit rate (share of events with CAR < −2%), and persistence of the drop. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage for small samples. Configurable weights. Sectors with N < 25 are marked “insufficient data.”

Is key-person risk priced?

Arguments that it should be: Firm-specific human capital cannot be fully diversified; sudden departures can signal governance risk; classic event studies find significant negative CARs (especially for founders and R&D-intensive firms); some cross-sectional evidence links key-person concentration to valuation discounts or higher expected returns.

Arguments that it may not be fully priced: Purely idiosyncratic risk should not be priced under standard asset-pricing models; markets may already anticipate departures via succession planning; announcements are often confounded with other news; long-horizon pricing evidence remains mixed.

The KPRI measures realized market reactions. Whether those reactions reflect a priced risk premium, information effects, or both is an open empirical question that the repository’s regression and robustness tests are designed to address. The index is descriptive, not a tradable factor.
